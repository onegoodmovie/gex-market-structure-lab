## 0. Terminology — two different outside parties, and the code calls both "vendor"

This document keeps them apart. Earlier documents do not, and §6 below was
written wrong because of it.

| | **Massive Options** | **optioncharts.io** |
|---|---|---|
| Role | the data source | the third-party reference |
| Supplies | raw chain: IV, greeks, OI | published netGEX, call/put GEX, flip, walls |
| Publishes GEX? | **no** — it has no such product | yes, and that is the whole point of it |
| Lives in | `massive_iv`, `massive_gamma`, `massive_vega` | `optioncharts_reference.csv`, `reference_net_gex` |
| Used by | Path A, §4 expiry offset, put-call IV consistency | Q1 only |

So "the vendor's expiry convention" (§4) is a statement about **Massive**, and
"the vendor's flip" (§5) is a statement about **optioncharts**. They are not the
same party and nothing measured against one transfers to the other without
saying so.

The collision was in the code, not just the prose. **Fixed on T1**, same day it
was found: `massive_*` for the data source, `reference_*` for optioncharts,
`provider_*` for genuinely provider-agnostic plumbing. It cost little because
`normalized/` and `derived/` are regenerable from `raw_chain/`, which keeps the
provider's own column names untouched by design — only the hand-maintained CSV
header was a real migration. Every downstream number reproduced bit-for-bit.

## 4. Massive's expiry offset, sharpened: constant at the short end, decaying with tenor

Everything in this section is **our convention vs Massive's**, measured from
Massive's own `vega / gamma = S²·σ·T`. optioncharts is not involved.

T0 measured +3.38 h median and read it as flat across tenor. Two more readings:

| Reading | median dT | range |
|---|---|---|
| T0 14:00 | +3.38 h | 2.84 – 3.90 |
| T1 09:45 | +3.33 h | 1.79 – 3.91 |
| T1 14:00 | **+2.80 h** | 2.00 – 3.81 |

The 14:00 median moved 0.58 h between days, which looks like instability. It is
mostly composition. Matched by DTE, the two 14:00 readings are close where it
matters and separate where it does not:

| DTE | T0 dT | T1 dT |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | +3.90 h | +3.81 h |
| 1 | +3.42 h | +3.46 h |
| 2 | +3.56 h | +3.28 h |
| 7 | +2.84 h | +2.14 h |

Median over DTE 0–2: **+3.56 h (T0) vs +3.46 h (T1) — six minutes apart.** The
median over all expiries fell only because T1's chain carried an extra
long-dated expiry, and the long end is exactly where dT is smallest and least
reproducible.

So the T0 statement "flat across tenor, +3.38 h" should be narrowed to: **dT
against Massive is ~3.5 h at 0–2 DTE and reproducible there to within minutes
across two days; it decays with tenor and is not reproducible past a week.**
That is a stronger claim about the part of the curve the study actually uses and
an honest retreat on the rest.

**One piece of T0's evidence has to be re-labelled.** T0 §4 offered the
optioncharts 16:22 reading — still reporting finite, *recomputed* Jul 27 gamma
22 minutes after PM settlement — as "corroboration from a different direction"
for this offset. It is evidence, but it is evidence about **optioncharts'**
expiry convention, used to support a measurement of **Massive's**. It only
corroborates if the two share a convention, which has never been tested. Read
it as a second, independent observation that *some* other party runs a later
expiry clock — not as confirmation of the number in this table.

`massive_expiry_offset_hours` **stays null.** Two days is not enough, and the
tenor decay means a single scalar is the wrong shape for the thing anyway.

Path A, unchanged in character: 1.98% median at 0945, 2.14% at 1400, both
"reported, not blocking". The 4c joint fit still reproduces the vendor's gamma
curve to 0.49–0.63% rms once their S and T are used.

## 6. The flip gap is not explained by Massive's expiry offset — but this test is weaker than it looks

**Read §0 first.** The candidate explanation for §5's item 3 was the §4 offset,
and the two are measured against *different parties*: dT is our clock vs
**Massive's**, while the flip gap is our number vs **optioncharts'**. Applying
one to the other silently assumes optioncharts and Massive share an expiry
convention. That assumption has never been tested and is not obviously true.

The test as run — re-solving our 0DTE flip with T shifted by the measured dT:

| dT applied | T0 flip vs vendor | T1 flip vs vendor |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 h | +10.98 | +7.44 |
| 2.8 h | +6.75 | +9.84 |
| **3.4 h** | **+5.64** | **+9.92** |
| 5.0 h | +3.30 | +9.55 |

It closes the gap on T0 and **widens** it on T1. A single correction cannot be
the explanation while moving in opposite directions on two days.

But the failure is **two-sided and this test cannot say which side**:

- optioncharts may simply not share Massive's expiry convention, in which case
  applying Massive's dT was never going to work and the result says nothing
  about the flip gap; or
- the expiry convention is not what drives the flip gap at all.

So the honest statement is narrower than "hypothesis killed": **shifting T by
Massive's measured offset does not move our flip onto optioncharts', and the
experiment cannot distinguish a wrong correction from a wrong cause.** Testing
it properly needs optioncharts' own implied T, which would have to come from
published greeks it does not appear to expose — so this may not be resolvable
on the current sources at all.

What survives independently of any of that: the **+7 to +11 point flip bias
itself**, same sign on two days whose net GEX had opposite signs. That is a
direct observation, not a modelled one, and it is the most interesting open
question in the study.

## 6b-bis. How much does a flip comparison actually prove? Measured.

Raised in review: a single-expiry flip is a curve's zero, so it may be blind to
whole-curve scaling — in which case §6b's 0.18 pts would prove far less than it
appears. Measured on the 14:00 capture rather than argued:

| | per hour of T error | per point of S error |
|---|---|---|
| 0DTE | 0.73 – 1.15 pts/h | **0.000, exactly** |
| 1DTE | 0.24 – 0.28 pts/h | **0.000, exactly** |
| 2DTE | 0.46 – 0.48 pts/h | **0.000, exactly** |

*(A −3.4 h shift on 0DTE drives T negative and was discarded.)*

**On spot the objection is right, and stronger than stated.** The flip is not
merely insensitive to the observation spot — it is *independent* of it by
construction. Under sticky strike the observed S never enters the exposure
curve; it only sets the solver's grid window. Verified exactly: the curve value
at a fixed trial spot is bit-identical across observation spots from 7400 to
7480. **A flip agreement is not evidence about S, and — the part that matters —
"0DTE spot uncertainty" cannot cause the 0DTE flip bias.** That was the standing
hypothesis after §6b. It is dead, on structure rather than on evidence.

**On T the objection does not hold.** The flip responds at 0.28 pts/h on 1DTE,
so agreement to 0.18 pts bounds any T difference to about **0.6 h** — a
tighter constraint on T than the vega/gamma route gives. A 3.4 h offset would
have moved it ~0.95 pts and been visible. The sensitivity is also **not
monotone in tenor**: 2DTE is twice as T-sensitive as 1DTE, because it depends on
each expiry's own OI distribution. It has to be measured per expiry, not
reasoned about from tenor.

**Where that leaves the 0DTE bias.** At 0.73–1.15 pts/h, the +7.44 pt gap needs
6–10 h of T difference against a measured offset of 3.4 h. So T is
quantitatively insufficient and S is structurally impossible — **both standing
suspects are excluded.** Remaining: IV/smile handling, OI, contract inclusion,
or the simplest one, that the two numbers are not the same object. Both
flip-bias readings came from the **screenshot headline**; the 0.18-pt agreement
came from the **zero of the published curve**. Whether optioncharts' headline
flip equals its own `gex_profile` root is a free check as soon as a 0DTE CSV and
a 0DTE headline exist for the same expiry — tomorrow.

## 6c. What the filter costs, measured — and why it is not repaired

Q1 is now **structural validation / discrepancy diagnosis**, provisionally
named. Not a calibration: optioncharts' spot, upstream source and outlier
handling are undisclosed, so this measures whether two independent
constructions behave alike, and confers no licence to tune ours onto theirs.

`scripts/compare_exposure_by_strike.py` runs the whole comparison and §6 of its
output measures what the implausible-greeks filter costs — **beside** the
number, never inside it.

The stand-in IV is the **same strike's opposite leg** (put-call parity, the
relation §3.2 already leans on). Two things it is deliberately not: not backed
out of optioncharts, which would launder the reference into the quantity being
tested against it; and not a smile interpolation, because there is no local
smile to interpolate — every Jul 29 put from 7530 up carries the identical
value **0.000379**, a sentinel, not a measurement. The calls at those same
strikes are out-of-the-money and invert cleanly at 13.3–16.3%.

Jul 29, 42 contracts excluded (OI 3,217), 37 with a usable opposite leg:

| | as reported | if restored | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| net GEX | −2.707B | −2.772B | −0.065B |
| flip | 7451.92 | 7453.21 | +1.29 |
| **flip gap vs optioncharts** | **+0.18** | **+1.47** | +1.29 |
| curve median abs diff | 0.494B | 0.336B | **−0.159B** |
| correlation | 0.99837 | 0.99846 | +0.00009 |

**Restoring improves the curve and worsens the flip.** Any repair chosen to fix
one would have quietly broken the other, and a repair chosen because it made the
two sides agree would have been fitted to the reference rather than measured
against it. This is the case for reporting the filter's cost instead of acting
on it, and it is now enforced in the script's own labelling.

## 6d. M3 built — and the study's original question has a first answer

`actual_map.py`, `attribution.py`, `run_actual_map.py`, 12 tests. The §12
identity gate passes on every row of both days. 95 tests total.

Two design decisions that the T1 findings forced, both of which would have
silently corrupted the result:

**One contract set across all four states.** §3 found that 331 contracts
implausible at 09:45 have good greeks by 14:00. Letting each state use its own
usable rows would put that membership change inside `D − A` and report it as
surface repricing. The intersection is used. What that costs is measured and
printed: on T1 the largest was 8-30DTE/flip, where the unconstrained D would
have differed by **−304% of that row's total change**. The artifact was bigger
than the signal.

**One bucket policy across all four states.** The §9.1 0DTE aggregate rules are
keyed on capture label, so a t1 of 15:45 would drop 0DTE from D's aggregate
while A kept it — the identity would fail on membership, not on attribution.
t1's policy is applied to all four (`recompute_at` gained a `policy_label`
argument; M2's behaviour is unchanged).

### Net GEX is almost entirely mechanical, and the surface damps it

All 16 net-GEX rows — two days × two windows × four buckets:

| | mechanical share of total change |
|---|---|
| min | 95% |
| **median** | **113%** |
| max | 133% |

And in **14 of 16 rows the surface contribution carries the opposite sign to
the total change.** On T0, net GEX fell and the surface term was positive; on
T1 it rose and the surface term was negative. Surface repricing *damps* the
spot/time move rather than adding to it — a regularity, on two days, that the
study had no way to see before today.

| date | window | bucket | total | mechanical | surface |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | 0945→1400 | aggregate | −41.48B | −51.28B | +9.81B |
| T0 | 0945→1545 | aggregate | −22.13B | −25.01B | +2.87B |
| T1 | 0945→1400 | aggregate | +35.95B | +39.72B | −3.78B |
| T1 | 0945→1545 | aggregate | +16.55B | +17.92B | −1.38B |

### The flip is the mirror image — and its mechanical part is pure time

For the flip the two components swap roles:

| date | window | bucket | total | mechanical | surface |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | 0945→1400 | 0DTE | −13.56 | +1.49 | **−15.05** |
| T0 | 0945→1400 | 8-30DTE | −5.44 | −0.01 | **−5.44** |
| T1 | 0945→1400 | 8-30DTE | −5.57 | +0.27 | **−5.84** |
| T1 | 0945→1545 | 8-30DTE | −4.66 | +0.39 | **−5.04** |

Mechanical never exceeds +4.28 points on any row; surface reaches −15. **Net
GEX moves for mechanical reasons and the flip moves for surface reasons.**

There is a structural reason the flip's mechanical term is so small, and it is
worth stating because it is not obvious: **the flip does not depend on spot at
all** (§6b-bis). Under sticky strike the observed S never enters the exposure
curve. So for the flip metric, `mechanical_component` is *pure time decay* —
spot contributes exactly zero, by construction. The 8-30DTE rows show this
cleanly: −0.01, +0.27, +0.39 points over four to six hours, which is all the
decay a 30-day contract has in an afternoon.

**Language discipline, per §12.** This is a counterfactual attribution under a
declared model. It is not a causal decomposition, and state C never existed in
the market. Every row carries `attribution_is_counterfactual`.

## 6e. The 09:45 base question turned into a measurement

§3 found that 09:45 is the capture exposed to Massive's unsettled open, and
every frozen-map row is built on it. Rather than choose between "move the base
to 10:30" and "keep 09:45 and carry the caveat", **a 10:30 capture was added**
and both bases are built. Calls are unlimited on this tier, so the extra
snapshot costs nothing.

- `com.spxgex.surface1030`, fires **10:45 ET** Mon–Fri (target as-of 10:30,
  derived from `provider.delay_minutes` like every other agent — installed and
  verified loaded). Six agents now.
- `1030` added to `capture.target_asof_times`, `frozen_map.recompute_times` and
  `actual_map.surface_times`. Everything else derives from those.
- 10:30 and 10:45 spots **backfilled for T0 and T1** from Yahoo, so the new
  recompute point is populated on the days already captured rather than
  permanently blank.
- `build_frozen_inputs` / `build_frozen_map` take a `surface_base` override;
  `run_frozen_map.py` gains `--base` and `--compare-base`. A base with no
  capture fails loudly rather than falling back. Both tested.

The written map still uses 09:45. **Changing that is a config change to be made
on several days of the comparison table, not on one.**

**One limitation, stated before the first number arrives.** The 0945-vs-1030
difference is the *total* consequence of the base choice, and it mixes the
09:45 transient with 45 minutes of genuine surface movement. It does not
isolate the transient — only the flag-set diff of §3 does that. Read the two
together, or a normal morning of vol drift will be mistaken for a data defect.
