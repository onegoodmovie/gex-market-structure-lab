## 4. The delay assumption is verified, and the flip bias survives — 0DTE only

This section was written once and then rewritten. The first version concluded
the flip bias was an artifact of as-of pairing. That was wrong, and the way it
was wrong is worth keeping: the pairing **was** wrong, but in the opposite
direction from the one assumed.

### 4a. Our 15-minute delay, verified for the first time

Every T in this study is inferred as `request_time − 15 min` (level 4, T0 §3.2),
and the study has recorded since T0 that the implied delay is "not identifiable
— Massive publishes no spot". But the §7.2 joint (S, T) fit *does* recover an
implied spot from Massive's own gamma curve, and T2's spot changed direction
three times, which makes the test sharp:

| capture | implied S | 30-min delay | **15-min (assumed)** | 0-min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0945 | 7425.00 | 10.0 | **1.6** | 24.4 |
| 1030 | 7373.64 | 6.3 | **1.9** | 8.4 |
| 1400 | 7397.51 | 19.3 | 12.1 | 5.0 |
| 1545 | 7334.79 | 43.7 | **2.4** | 18.6 |
| **total abs error** | | **79.2** | **18.0** | **56.4** |

**`provider.delay_minutes: 15` is correct**, to 15-minute resolution, on
evidence entirely internal to our own data. The 1400 capture is the single
outlier — and it is the same capture that failed the NTM gate. The study's
standing reading, "a level, not a lag", survives for the other three.

### 4b. optioncharts' exposure page is not 15 minutes delayed

The dashboard's headline 1DTE gamma flip read **7431.50** at the 14:00 download
and **7422.81** at 14:15 — matching the two exports' `gex_profile` zeros
exactly. Two things follow.

**The headline flip *is* the curve zero.** That closes the T1 open question of
whether the two are the same object. They are, to the last decimal. So T0's and
T1's headline-based readings and T2's curve-based readings are one series.

**And our 14:00 capture pairs with their 14:00 download, not their 14:15 one:**

| | ours, as-of 14:00 | their **14:00** download | their 14:15 download |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1DTE flip | 7430.95 | **7431.50 → −0.55** | 7422.81 → +7.90 |
| 0DTE flip | 7420.70 | **7415.61 → +5.09** | 7410.20 → +10.50 |
| 0DTE gamma ratio | — | **1.0043** | 1.1635 |
| 1DTE curve corr | — | **0.99975** | 0.99900 |

Given §4a fixes our as-of at 14:00, their 14:00 download must carry ~14:00
data. **Their exposure page is effectively live despite the "15 min delay"
label** — which presumably refers to quotes, or is a generic site disclaimer.

This is an inference that contradicts the site's own label, so it is not
adopted: it is re-tested every day by downloading at both 14:00 and 14:15, which
costs one click and is what made today resolvable at all.

### 4d. Why the wrong pairing was so convincing

Pairing our 14:00 capture against their 14:15 export moved the 1DTE gap from
−0.55 to +7.90. **Fifteen minutes of surface is worth ~8 flip points** — which
is the same order as the 0DTE bias being investigated. M3 established the flip
is surface-driven; §3 above measures 14–18 points per 45 minutes. The lesson
holds even though the conclusion reversed: **a flip comparison is worthless
without the as-of pinned on both sides**, and it was only pinned today because
the export was taken twice.

## 5c. Three tests run in review — the σ(K) one lands

### 5c-i. The `median 1.0048` was hiding a one-sided tail. It is σ(K) shape.

Raised in review: the same line that read `median 1.0048` also read `p10 0.79`
and `p90 1.03`. That is not symmetric noise around 1 — it is a one-sided tail,
and reading it as noise is the trap this study wrote down on T0 (*the median
hides the thing that matters*). Two compounding errors made it invisible:
**`compare_exposure_by_strike.py` §3 only ever computed the ratio for calls**,
and it restricted to a ±1–2% band, which is narrower than the tail.

Recomputed per strike, both legs, usable contracts only, OI > 100, on the
correctly-paired export:

| ln(K/S) | 0DTE call γ | σ_oc/σ_mv | 0DTE put γ | σ_oc/σ_mv |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| −0.040 .. −0.020 | — | — | 1.093 | 0.915 |
| −0.010 .. −0.003 | 1.259 | 0.794 | 0.876 | 1.142 |
| −0.003 .. +0.003 | 1.078 | 0.928 | 0.881 | 1.136 |
| +0.010 .. +0.020 | 0.875 | 1.143 | 1.044 | 0.957 |
| +0.020 .. +0.040 | 0.791 | 1.264 | 0.622 | 1.609 |

Slope of the gamma ratio against `ln(K/S)`:

| | 0DTE | 1DTE |
|---|---|---|
| call | **−9.56** | **−8.36** |
| put | **−4.63** | **−1.22** |

**All four negative, monotone across seven moneyness bands, on both legs and
both tenors.** optioncharts' implied σ runs ~20% *below* Massive's beneath spot
and 15–55% *above* it overhead — **a systematically flatter skew.** This is a
convention difference in the one place the study had not looked, and it was
declared excluded on the strength of a median.

**It also predicts the flip sign and the tenor dependence.** Near the crossing
the 0DTE ATM put ratio is 0.881 — optioncharts' put gamma is lower there, so
their put leg is less negative, their curve sits higher, and their zero moves
left. Their flip is lower. Ours is higher. That is the observed sign on all
three days. At 1DTE the same band reads 0.971, an order of magnitude closer to
1 — and the 1DTE flip gap is −0.55 against 0DTE's +5.09.

Sign consistency and tenor dependence both fall out. "0DTE gamma is unstable"
predicts neither: instability is random and would not hold a sign for three days.

**Item 5 moves from "this tier cannot resolve it" to "located: σ(K) shape
difference between the two upstream sources, measured."** It is not fixable —
optioncharts' σ is not published and this is a difference between two vendors'
smiles, not an error in ours — but it is now named rather than mysterious.

One caveat: the put slope is **not robust to a 15-minute mispairing** (it reads
+4.66 on the wrongly-paired export against −4.63 on the right one). The
measurement needs the as-of pinned, which §4b only just made possible.

### 5c-ii. The "wandering" degradation is deterministic moneyness migration

The 411 contracts clean at 09:45 and flagged by 14:00, ranked by their moneyness
*at 09:45*:

| | already bad at 09:45 | **newly bad by 14:00** | clean at both |
|---|---|---|---|
| median \|ln(K/S)\| | 0.185 | **0.070** | 0.040 |

**99% of the newly-flagged are in-the-money.** They sit in a band strictly
between the always-clean core and the always-bad deep wing — the edge of the
failure zone. Spot fell 54 points intraday, contracts migrated deeper ITM, and
the IV inversion failed on them in the order their moneyness predicts.

**So §2's "the degradation wanders" is wrong as an explanation.** It is not
supplier jitter. It is a deterministic consequence of moneyness migrating with
spot across a fixed failure boundary. Open item 8 can be closed, and open item
10 answered: the NTM gate fires on days spot moves, which is not a defect in
the gate but a property of the chain.

### 5c-iii. The vol-drift hypothesis for 8-30DTE — tested, and rejected

Alternative proposed in review: the 8-30DTE exception is intraday vol drift
pushing one direction regardless of market direction, and 8-30DTE carries the
largest vega/gamma so it feels it most. Test: does each bucket's surface
contribution sign follow its *own* ATM IV change from t0 to t1?

| bucket | sign agreement |
|---|---|
| 1-7DTE | **6 / 6** |
| 0DTE | 3 / 6 |
| **8-30DTE** | **2 / 6** |

**Rejected, and informatively.** For 1-7DTE the mechanism is exact — six for six,
the surface contribution follows ATM vol level. For 8-30DTE it is worse than
chance and *anti*-correlated on T1 and T2: IV fell and the surface contribution
was positive, IV rose and it was negative.

So the 8-30DTE exception is not an ATM-level effect. The remaining candidate is
smile *shape* rather than level — that bucket spans strikes far from ATM, where
an ATM median says least. Not chased: §7 explains why.
