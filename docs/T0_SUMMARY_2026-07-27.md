## 3. Problems found, and what was done

### 3.1 cron cannot run this study

`security find-generic-password` fails under cron — a cron job runs in a
security session with no access to the login keychain. Measured:

```
cron         keychain: FAIL   LaunchAgent  keychain: OK
```

Switched to LaunchAgents. Then a second wall: this repo is under `~/Documents`,
which macOS protects with TCC, and TCC grants access **per executable**:

```
/bin/bash                 read ~/Documents: FAIL,  exec script: EPERM
~/miniconda3/bin/python3  read: OK,  write: OK
```

So each agent execs the interpreter directly with the capture script as an
argument. `run_capture.sh` is off the scheduled path. Both schedulers also hand
the job `PATH=/usr/bin:/bin`, where `python3` has none of our dependencies, so
the real interpreter is baked into each plist. All four links were verified by
firing a real agent, not assumed.

### 3.2 Every T in this study is inferred

The §5.3 as-of ladder finds nothing usable:

| Level | Field | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `last_quote.last_updated` | absent — no quotes on Starter |
| 2 | `underlying_asset.last_updated` | absent — the block holds only `ticker` |
| 3 | `day.last_updated` | **rejected** — 138 values spanning 364 days; it is each contract's last *trade* date |
| 4 | `request_time − 15 min` | **what we actually use** |

The resolver now rejects any candidate whose values span more than an hour
across contracts. Consequence: `asof_is_inferred = true` on every row, the delay
assumption is load-bearing, and **download punctuality propagates straight into
T** — a 15:45 capture firing 3 minutes late puts a 20% error in a 0DTE T.

### 3.3 Massive returns no spot

`underlying_asset` carries only `ticker`. `transmission_spot.csv` is therefore
not a fallback but the *only* spot source. Spot is matched to each capture's
**resolved as-of**, not to its label — today's 09:45 capture resolved to 09:52
while spot moved 22 points in 15 minutes, and label-matching would have paired a
surface and a spot seven minutes apart. Nearest recorded instant only, never
interpolated; a gap wider than 5 minutes is a severe flag.

### 3.4 Impossible vendor greeks, concentrated near expiry

`gamma < 0` on 5.6% of contracts, `vega < 0` on 6.2%, IV below 1% on 11.0% — all
residue of IV inversion failing deep in the money (43.8% of strikes >15% ITM,
0% of OTM). Flagged `implausible_greeks`, excluded from every computed quantity,
never dropped from the record.

The loss grows through the session, and almost all of it is 0DTE:

| Capture | NTM OI lost | 0DTE bucket |
|---|---|---|
| 09:45 | 0.17% | 1.45% |
| 14:00 | 0.42% | 2.51% |
| 15:45 | 1.15% | 8.78% |

The gate is OI-weighted (GEX-weighted agrees to within 0.12pp) and applies only
to the layers a row actually reports — at 15:45 the 0DTE layer is already held
out of the aggregate, so its degradation must not invalidate the layers that
are reported. Without that scoping the 15:45 capture fails every day for a
reason unrelated to the numbers it carries.

optioncharts.io, Jul 27 0DTE row:

| Reading | Net GEX | Call GEX |
|---|---|---|
| 14:00 | −5.41B | +8.70B |
| **15:45** | **+191.27B** | **+956.32B** |

this row would have entered the study as fact.

## 4. The main finding: the vendor's expiry is ~3.4 hours later than ours

Path A fails at ~1.7% median on both captures, and no `r − q` brings it under
1%. The residual by bucket on the 14:00 capture: 8-30DTE −1.2%, 1-7DTE +4.9%,
**0DTE −78%**.

**The median hides it completely.** Long-dated contracts dominate the chain and
hours of expiry offset are invisible to them: a correcting offset moves the
median 1.68% → 1.84% while p90 collapses 78.8% → 6.4%. §7.1 specifies the median
as the gate, and optimising it concludes "no offset" and is badly wrong.

**Measurement.** From the vendor's own greeks, `vega / gamma = S²·σ·T`, which
isolates T:

| Expiry | Our T | Their T | dT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 27 (0DTE) | 2.00 h | 5.90 h | +3.90 h |
| Jul 28 | 26.00 h | 29.42 h | +3.42 h |
| Jul 29 | 49.99 h | 53.55 h | +3.56 h |
| Jul 31 | 98.00 h | 101.27 h | +3.27 h |
| Aug 3 | 170.00 h | 172.83 h | +2.84 h |

Flat across tenor, median **+3.38 h**. A constant offset is the signature of a
fixed expiry-*time* convention — not a delay, not a day count.

**Two false trails, both worth recording.**

*A gamma-only fit lies here.* Near the money gamma depends on σ and T only
through `σ√T`, so fitting T against the gamma curve absorbs any σ-side
difference into T. Such a fit produced a dT that fell steadily with tenor and
crossed zero at 4.6 days — a convincing, reproducible, and entirely spurious
"day-count effect". Vega carries √T outside the ratio and breaks the
degeneracy. The gamma fit is kept as a cross-check, not as the measurement.

*Peak location overstates the spot.* Gamma carries a 1/σ factor, so a
downward-sloping smile pushes the apparent peak above S — measured at +1 to +4
points today. Feeding the vendor's own σ(K) into a fit removes the bias.

**Corroboration from a different direction.** At 16:22, 22 minutes after PM
settlement, the vendor still reported a finite Jul 27 gamma, *recomputed* rather
than cached (flip 7409.41 → 7412.73, call wall 7400 → 7415). Under our 16:00 ET
settlement those contracts have negative T; under a convention ~3.5 h later they
still have hours of life.

**Not written into config.** `validation.vendor_expiry_offset_hours` stays null.
One day's readings span 2.84–3.90 h. That establishes *that* a constant offset
exists and roughly where; it does not name a number. Per §19.4, reporting beats
picking the closest fit.

**What it does not change.** Our own GEX keeps 16:00 ET — that is the correct PM
settlement instant, and the vendor differing does not make us wrong. The offset
lives only inside Path A, whose job is to separate our formula from their
conventions. Per §8.2 this is a `definition_gap`, not a `numerical_error`.

Once their S and T are used, our formula reproduces their gamma curve to
**0.7% rms**. The failure is convention, not arithmetic.

## 5. Is the GEX number inside the 15-minute delay?

Partly, and the part that matters is not the delayed part.

| Input | Delayed? |
|---|---|
| IV / greeks | **yes** — derived from delayed option prices |
| Open interest | no — OCC overnight, a different thing entirely |
| Spot S | **no**, on the evidence below |
| T | computed from someone's clock |

The spot test is the sign, not the size. Fitting the vendor's implied S gives
+2.75 / +2.14 / +1.14 points across the three captures — all positive. The
14:15→15:45 leg **rose** (7396.32 → 7411.86); a delayed spot must read *low* on
a rising leg. It read high. A delay changes sign with the market; this does not.
The residual +1 to +3 points is a level, most likely forward/dividend handling.

So the 15-minute delay is real for the greeks, and we handle it by shifting the
download clock. But it is a 15-minute problem sitting next to a **3.4-hour**
convention problem — an order of magnitude larger.

## 6. Still open

   T-bill, SOFR and dividend yield were never entered, so §7.2 had nothing to
   search.
2. **Exact expiry offset** — 2.84–3.90 h on one day. Needs several days.
3. **The ~2% uniform residual** after the offset is applied — uniform across
   buckets, so a scale effect rather than a time effect. `r`, `q` and the
   discount factor are the remaining suspects.
4. **0DTE gamma cannot be validated on this tier at all.** A 10-point spot
   uncertainty is a 6% gamma error at 6 hours to expiry and **75%** at 15
   minutes. Massive publishes no spot; ours is hand-recorded at 1-minute
   granularity from another source. Path A can validate 8-30DTE, weakly
   1-7DTE, and not 0DTE.
5. **Which vendor row basis to standardise on** — today's readings mixed a
   single-expiry row and a 5-expiry aggregate, and neither matches the study's
   DTE≤45 aggregate.
