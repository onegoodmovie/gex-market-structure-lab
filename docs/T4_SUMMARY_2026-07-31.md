## 2. Two bugs, both found only because of the roll-off

### 2a. The structure guard was a false alarm

`compare_exposure_by_strike.py` §1 refused today's 0DTE export with *"legs do
not sum to net — the export's meaning has changed, stop here"*. It had not. The
largest residual was **0.01** — the export rounds all three columns
independently to cents. The guard used `np.allclose`, whose default is
*relative*, and on far-wing strikes whose net is itself a few cents a
one-cent residual fails a relative test. Today's 479-strike export is full of
such rows; a 244-strike one is not.

Replaced with an absolute cent tolerance (0.05), which is what rounding to two
decimals actually bounds. **A guard that blocks a real day is worse than no
guard**, and this one would have silently cost the roll-off comparison.

### 2b. Our flip was being solved at *their* download spot

The comparison passed `--spot` — the export's download spot — into
`solve_flip`. That spot centres the solver's grid, and a different grid phase
can step over a narrow sign excursion and return a different root. On today's
0DTE the same 15:45 capture reported a flip of **7450.11 or 7423.74** purely
from which spot was passed in, a 26-point swing, and the 16:00 comparison was
reported as −35.84 when it is −9.47.

Our flip must depend only on our capture. Fixed: it is now solved at the
capture's own transmission spot, and the export's spot is used only where it
belongs — the gamma backout and the comparison band.

**Checked against the study's own outputs: zero rows change.** The pipeline
always passed each capture its own spot, so no published number was ever
affected. The bug lived only in the comparison script.

## 3. The roll-off finding: the 0DTE flip is not well defined today

Our 0DTE exposure curve at 15:45 crosses zero **three times inside 27 points** —
7423.7, 7445.6, 7450.1. The curve dips negative only between about 7446 and
7451, an excursion narrower than the solver's 5-point grid, so the default grid
finds either three roots or one depending on phase.

`flip_solver.grid_step_points: 5` carries the comment *"must stay finer than the
25-pt SPX strike spacing"*. **On the roll-off day the near-money strikes are
5 points apart, so the parameter equals the spacing rather than beating it.** At
step 2 or finer all three roots are found from any phase.

The value was **not changed**: re-solving all five days at step 1.0 moves
**zero** flips, so this is a latent fragility rather than an error in anything
reported. It is recorded because it is exactly the sort of thing that only
appears on a day like this, and the next roll-off will meet it again.

The comparison script now prints a warning when the curve has more than one
root, so "the flip" is never quoted as a single number without saying so.

## 4. The as-of test does not discriminate today

Three download times against two captures. The two hypotheses predict opposite
orderings — under *live*, the same-instant pairing is 15:45↔15:45; under
*15-min delayed*, it is 14:15↔14:00 and 16:00↔15:45.

| pairing | 1DTE gap | 0DTE gap |
|---|---|---|
| their 14:15 vs our 14:00 | **+1.97** | −7.04 |
| their 15:45 vs our 15:45 | +3.58 | **−6.45** |
| their 16:00 vs our 15:45 | +2.78 | −9.47 |

**1DTE favours "delayed"** (the two delayed-same-instant pairings are the two
closest). **0DTE favours "live"** (the live-same-instant pairing is closest).
They contradict, and both spreads are small — 1.6 points on 1DTE, 3.0 on 0DTE.

**So the stop condition is not met.** T2 discriminated strongly, T3 weakly, T4
not at all. The double download was to run until three consecutive confirmations;
it got one. The manual phase ends today regardless, so this closes as
**unresolved rather than confirmed** — and the T2/T3 conclusion that
optioncharts' exposure page is effectively live keeps its "inference, not
observation" label.
